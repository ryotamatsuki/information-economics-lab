# Round 3 — model and mathematical results (reconstructed)

## Primitives
N=2M risk-neutral firms, equal numbers in fixed portfolios A and B; signs a_i=+1 or -1. Finite identical regions K>=2. Independent Gaussian fundamentals theta_i with variance T=tau^2>0, common region shock variance r>0 and independent idiosyncratic noise variance s>=0. y_i=theta_i+epsilon_g(i)+eta_i. Firms choose location before seeing private fundamental information. Investor must choose one portfolio based on public signals. Binding signed cash-flow share 0<beta<1 of each selected firm's own realized fundamental; no exit or limited liability. Let h=T+s, kappa=h/r and c=T^2/h.

For region n_g and imbalance S_g=sum a_i, q=N-sum_g S_g^2/(kappa+n_g), x_i=1-a_i*S_g(i)/(kappa+n_g(i)), Q=c*q, investment value V=sqrt(Q/(2*pi)), firm gain B_i=beta*sqrt(c/(2*pi))*x_i/sqrt(q). Total firm share beta*V and investor share (1-beta)*V. q is a specific portfolio-comparison objective, not a universal Blackwell ordering. At fixed profile, portfolio choices have equal unconditional probability in the symmetric model, so fixed prize cannot replicate the same incentives.

## P1: scale bias
If q_-i denotes explained contrast variance normalized by c when i's signal is removed, and d=kappa+n_g, q-q_-i=d/(d-1)*x_i^2. Thus normalized payoff squared u_i^2=((d-1)/d)*(1-q_-i/q). For a unilateral relocation q_-i is fixed. Strict private improvement with q'<q requires n_destination_after>n_origin_before, hence sum_g n_g^2 increases. This is a necessary condition, not sufficient. Six-firm regression: A=[0,1,2], B=[0,1,1], A1 moves 0 to 1, kappa=1.25. q=814/153 -> 46/9, x_i=1 unchanged. V=.82304114 -> .80670123, B_i=.07734969 -> .07891642. See Stage 1 and 4A logs. Eight-firm extension can preserve product market profit and travel under the harmful move; do not equate V and welfare generally.

## P2: efficient pure location equilibria
NE iff all S_g=0 for information-only baseline. Since sum_i x_i=q<=N with strict inequality off balance, a minimum-x player has u_i<1/sqrt(N) if unbalanced. It is a majority-type player and can move to an opposite-surplus region, yielding x_i'>=1 and q'<=N, so strict gain. Conversely at balance an origin has n>=2 and destination m>=0. Let A=kappa+n-1, B=kappa+m+1>1. q'=N-1/A-1/B, x_i'=1-1/B. B/A<=m+1<=N-1; consequently q'-N*x_i'^2>0. All balanced profiles are strict NE. Empty regions included. No uniqueness claimed.

## P3: weak acyclicity
D(L)=sum_g |S_g|/2<=M; repeat opposite-surplus moves using a minimum-x player. Each chosen strict improvement reduces D by exactly 1 and increases q (majority departure lowers origin penalty; minority arrival lowers destination penalty). Hence a strict improvement path of length at most M exists from every profile. This does not prove every strict improvement path terminates. FIP remains conjectural; candidate log potential rejected. Exact and floating grid checks support but do not prove stronger claims.

## P4: four-firm two-location product-market extension
Two rival pairs (A1,B1), (A2,B2), each a covered unit consumer market on [0,1], quadratic transport t>=0, cost zero, nonnegative prices unrestricted above. Consumers must buy one product (no outside option). Locations 0 or 1. Investment payoff independent of price conditional on location. With separated rivals and t>0, D_1=clip((p_2-p_1+t)/(2t),0,1), BR_1=(p_2+t)/2 for p_2<=3t and p_2-t otherwise. Any player can secure positive product profit with p=t/2, hence both shares positive at equilibrium; unique prices t,t. With co-location or t=0, homogeneous Bertrand unique equilibrium prices 0,0; tie demand allocation arbitrary with same payoff/travel in these cases. Investor chooses larger posterior expectation, arbitrary at ties (probability zero when Q>0). Strictly, with private investor signals one should specify Bayesian sequential rationality and measurable strategies; the complete perfect-public-history continuation characterization is not a claim about every arbitrary imperfect-information SPNE concept. No outside-option/participation/limited-liability extension certified.

Let b=beta*sqrt(c/(2*pi)), lambda=t/(2*b), q3=4-1/(kappa+1)-1/(kappa+3). Set uS=kappa/((kappa+1)*sqrt(q3)), uL=(kappa+2)/((kappa+3)*sqrt(q3)), uH=(kappa+4)/((kappa+3)*sqrt(q3)), uP=.5*sqrt(kappa/(kappa+2)). Delta=.5-uL, alpha=.5-uS, gamma=uH-uP, 0<delta<alpha<gamma. All 16 binary profiles partition: all-together 2 iff lambda<=alpha; paired rivals 2 iff lambda<=delta; crossed 2 always; portfolio-separated 2 iff lambda>=gamma; 3+1 none. Thresholds inclusive. Count is supported location profiles, not count of full contingent strategies including ties. r=0: 4 rival-separated profiles with t>0, all 16 with t=0.
